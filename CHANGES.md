# Changes to what is recorded

- 2026-11-10: first version. Binance.US all USDT/USDC/USD pairs (trades complete from 2026-04-22, hourly book snapshot, 24 h stats); Coinbase and Kraken one-second reference bars from the first run; Coinbase one-minute candles back to 2026-04-22; daily probes and fee-page hashes.
- 2026-11-10b: Binance.US universe narrowed to pairs thin when first seen (24 h count at most 5,000; stop above 50,000), applied first on 2026-11-10, so busy pairs such as BTCUSDT and ETHUSDT stop after their first-run backfill of 3,000 trades. Book snapshots follow the universe; 24 h stats now cover listed pairs only. New: Binance.US daily candles with trade counts for every listed pair; Kalshi perps crypto market snapshot each run. Coinbase one-minute history now only for the five fixed bases and bases whose Binance.US pair averaged 50 or more trades a day in some month. Kraken limited to 15 bases. Reason: the full-universe backfill would have passed 1 GB, and busy pairs are not what the resting-bid test studies.
- 2026-11-10c: Binance.US best bid and ask (`data/binanceus/quotes/`), polled every 5 s by the chain's otherwise idle wait job, for every listed leg of each universe base: full snapshots every 10 minutes and best-price changes between them, about 20 of every 30 minutes. No new runner time: the wait job already ran until the next slot. Reason: every closed FAITH idea had to infer fills from other people's prints; quotes let crossed USD/USDT/USDC legs and a maker re-test be judged on what was actually offered.
- 2026-11-10d: quotes budget is now per session (about 800 kB, so at most about 40 MB a day) instead of 60 MB a day, so a cap never removes the same hours every day; failed polls (`x`) and the cap (`c`) are marked in the file.
- 2026-11-10e: Kalshi perps funding-rate history (public endpoint, all markets), fetched once a day and kept as served in `data/kalshi/funding/history.json`. Reason: FAITH's row M5 (onshore funding carry) assumes funding averages near zero without having measured it.
- 2026-11-10f: quotes are written one gzip file per session (`quotes/<day>/<HHMMSS>Z-<run>.ndjson.gz`) and the session budget is 3 MB raw instead of 800 kB. Reason: the first session (17:09 to 17:36 UTC) wrote 818 kB raw and hit the cap after 13 of its 27 minutes, so half of each session would have been snapshots only; gzip takes a session to about a quarter (189 kB for that one), so a full session costs about 0.4 MB committed, near 20 MB a day. gzip files do not delta-compress, so the repository grows by about that much a day (about 1 GB in 40 days): decide by 2026-12-10 whether to drop the busiest legs, poll less often or move old days to release assets.
- 2026-11-11: fix a quotes test that expected the local run id and so failed on the runner from 2026-11-10 18:07 to 2026-11-11 18:10 UTC. The failed tests stopped the record job in all 47 runs of that span, so nothing but quotes was recorded then; the first good run (18:07 UTC, commit 87b98a5) caught up Binance.US trades for both days. The hourly book snapshots and the 24 h stats of that span are missing (the first 2026-11-11 snapshot is at 18:08 UTC). One-second Coinbase bars were rebuilt from the trade backfill only as far back as its page limit reached: for SOL the whole span, for BTC only from 2026-11-11 14:16 UTC (2026-11-10 17:37 to 2026-11-11 14:16 is missing); other bases and Kraken were not checked one by one.
- 2026-11-11b: one-off Coinbase five-minute candles for BTC, ETH, SOL, XRP and DOGE, 2021-01 to 2025-12 (`data/coinbase/candles5m/`, gzip per base and month, started by hand). Reason: FAITH's intraday screens need years before 2026, and 2026 is FAITH's holdout, so the recorded one-minute candles (all 2026) cannot be used to design or test them. Roughly 10 MB gzipped per base.
- 2026-11-11c: Coinbase Exchange and Kraken spot best bid and ask for BTC, ETH, SOL, XRP and DOGE against USD (`data/coinbase/quotes/`, `data/kraken/quotes/`), polled every 5 s by the same wait job as Binance.US, in the same rows and one gzip file per venue per session. Coinbase reads `/products/{id}/book?level=1` per product (1 req/s against a public limit of 10 per IP); Kraken reads all five in one `/0/public/Ticker` call (0.2 req/s against public guidance of 1 per IP). Each venue polls in its own loop with its own 250 kB raw session budget, so a failure or rate limit on one never stops another or Binance.US; Binance.US files are unchanged. Expected growth about 1 to 2 MB a day gzipped for both together (worst case every price changes every poll: about 1.2 MB Coinbase, 0.8 MB Kraken). Reason: the 2026-12-10 resting-order verdict should cover more than one venue, so a fill judged on Binance.US can be checked against what the two biggest US venues offered at the same moments.
- 2026-11-11d: one-off Binance USD-M perpetual history for BTCUSDT, ETHUSDT, SOLUSDT and DOGEUSDT, 2021-01 to 2025-12, from Binance's public archive (`data/binance-um/`, started by hand, every archive checked against its sha256): five-minute candles with quote volume, trade count and taker-buy volume; funding rates per year; five-minute open interest with long/short ratios per month. Missing months or days are listed in each `missing.json`. Reason: offshore perp candles, funding and open interest as signals for US-spot research; research data only (no account, no key), and any trading stays on US venues. 2026 is FAITH's holdout and is refused by the script. Fetched in run 43082113898 (BTCUSDT klines, funding and metrics 2021-01 to 2021-11 in run 43081535662): 124 MB gzipped in all, klines 64 MB, metrics 61 MB, funding 0.2 MB, so the ratio columns were kept. Gaps: the archive has no daily metrics before 2021-12 for ETHUSDT, SOLUSDT and DOGEUSDT (BTCUSDT from 2021-01); SOLUSDT monthly klines lack 2022-02-26 to 02-28 and 2022-04-01 to 04-02; metrics rows are 99.9% of five-minute slots; the top-trader ratios and the taker ratio are empty (null) from about 2022-01 to 2022-12 (only `accountLS` is served then), so ratio studies should start in 2023.
- 2026-11-11e: `tools/maker-sim/` and the hand-started `maker-sim` workflow: FAITH's resting-order fill simulator (copied from FAITH with its source commit in each header) run on Tardis.dev's free first-of-month tick data (quotes and trades) for Coinbase BTC-USD, ETH-USD, SOL-USD and Kraken XBT-USD, ETH-USD, SOL-USD, 2021-01 to 2023-12. Raw Tardis files are downloaded into the runner's temp dir and deleted after each day; they are never committed (size, and Tardis's terms). Only small per-pair summaries are committed, under `data/derived/tardis-maker/<name>/<venue>-<symbol>/` (`summary.json`, `days.json`, `manifest.json` with each raw file's size and sha256; well under 1 MB a pair). Days in 2024 to 2026 are refused by the script (2026 is FAITH's holdout). Its tests sit in `tools/maker-sim/` and are not part of the record job's `test/*.test.mjs`. Reason: the thin-pair maker read (FAITH queue-prov-r1) asked whether deep pairs on the big US venues fill differently, and the 5 s polls cannot answer that; tick data can.
- 2026-11-11f: `maker-sim` workflow gains a `lead` mode (`tools/maker-sim/lead.mjs`): a feed-order diagnostic of how far Tardis's book rows lead or lag the prints (local timestamps), written as one small line a day to `data/derived/tardis-maker/<name>/<venue>-<symbol>/lead.ndjson`. No fill or markout is computed. Reason: FAITH queue-prov-r2's simulator assumes the two streams disagree by at most 250 ms, and its Coinbase calibration showed 46% of prints at a price the last book row had already left.
- 2026-11-11h: forward record for FAITH's P-OF3 q2 forward check (`recorder/forward.mjs`, a step of the record job). Coinbase Exchange five-minute candles for USDT, BTC, ETH, SOL and DOGE against USD, one file per base and whole UTC day, `data/forward/coinbase/candles5m/<BASE>/<YYYY-MM-DD>.ndjson.gz`, rows as `backfill.mjs` writes them. Only days from 2026-11-01, the first day after FAITH's 2026 holdout (2026-01-01 to 2026-10-31), and only once a day has ended plus 15 minutes, so a written file is final; earlier days are refused in code. A day on disk is skipped, so each record run fills only what is missing (at most 40 days a base a run). Public endpoint, no key. Reason: a free forward record for a FAITH screen that was killed on history; research only; nothing trades.
- 2026-11-11g: P-OF2 data preparation (`recorder/pof2.mjs`, hand-started `pof2` workflow). A universe job picks coins by a fixed rule from metadata and existence probes only (no price, return or open-interest value is read) and commits `data/universe/pof2.json` with the rule, each step's candidates and why each dropped out, the final list and the run id. A backfill job then fetches, for the final list in universe order, Coinbase five-minute candles (`backfill.mjs`, same format) and Binance USD-M daily metrics, 2022-01 to 2025-12, one commit per coin, stopping before any coin that would take all P-OF2 data past 150 MB gzipped. Metrics for these coins keep two columns, `[ms, sumOpenInterest]` (new `--metrics-cols sumoi`), not the seven of BTCUSDT, ETHUSDT, SOLUSDT and DOGEUSDT, to save space. Coverage from timestamps only goes to `data/universe/pof2-coverage.json`. Reason: open-interest research data for a possible FAITH study; research only; trading stays on US venues; 2026 is FAITH's holdout. Universe (run 43089445553): 203 Binance.US USD/USDT bases, 194 after exclusions, 56 with a Coinbase USD product and 5m candles on 2022-01-01 and 2025-12-01, 42 with USD-M metrics on 2022-01-01 and 2025-12-31 (only SHIB trades under another perp symbol, 1000SHIBUSDT), cut to the 15 earliest perp archives: ADA, BCH, ETC, LINK, LTC, XLM, ATOM, BAT, DASH, XTZ, ZEC, ALGO, COMP, KNC, ZRX (all `<BASE>USDT`). Renamed coins drop out at step b because Coinbase's product under the new name starts after 2022-01 (POL, ex-MATIC; S, ex-FTM; RENDER, ex-RNDR; SKY, ex-MKR); aliases were not followed. Fetched in run 43089887829, with LTC 2023-10 (a Coinbase timeout) filled in run 43099687978: 130.1 MB gzipped in all (Coinbase candles 83.7 MB, metrics 46.5 MB), under the 150 MB limit, so no coin was stopped for size. All 48 months are present for every coin and source. Coinbase five-minute slots with a candle: 0.999 for ADA, BCH, LINK, LTC, XLM; 0.92 to 0.99 for ETC, ATOM, ALGO; 0.57 (KNC) to 0.91 for the rest; Coinbase writes no candle for five minutes without a trade, so the low shares are mostly quiet minutes, not outages. The longest Coinbase stretch without a candle is about 6 hours from 2025-10-25 15:15 UTC on every coin. Metrics: hours with rows at both T and T-1h 0.999 or more for every coin; every coin's metrics lack about 10 hours from 2024-02-16 13:35 UTC; XLMUSDT and ZECUSDT lack 2023-12-13 and XTZUSDT 2023-12-08 (in their `missing.json`). Coverage, from timestamps only, in `data/universe/pof2-coverage.json`.
- 2026-11-12: quote session files (`data/binanceus/quotes/`, `data/coinbase/quotes/`, `data/kraken/quotes/`) and one-second bars (`data/coinbase/bars1s/`, `data/kraken/bars1s/`) move out of git into release assets, one `tar.gz` per stream kind, run and UTC day, in the release `tape-<day>` (`recorder/offload.mjs`, a step before each commit step). Each bundle gets one line in `manifests/<day>.ndjson`; files leave the checkout only after the asset is seen in the release at its size, and if anything fails they are committed to git as before. `recorder/materialize.mjs` rebuilds the files byte for byte, checking every sha256. Files committed before the switch stay in git; a bar day file committed before it gets its later rows as appended chunks. Nothing about the rows changes. Readers must materialize before reading these streams. Reason: these streams were about 27 MB a day of the repository's 31 MB a day packed growth (557 MB on 2026-11-11), which would pass GitHub's recommended 1 GB around 2026-11-25 and 5 GB around 2027-04; this settles the 2026-12-10 repo-size decision the 2026-11-10f entry set. A run cut off between upload and commit leaves an asset no manifest names; `materialize.mjs --check` lists them.
- 2026-11-12b: quote bridge across the half-hourly handover (`.github/workflows/bridge.yml`, `quotes.mjs --stream quotes-bridge`). The chain's quote sessions stop a minute before each slot and the next run's start about a minute after it; on 2026-11-11 the gap between Binance.US sessions was 105 to 111 s at every normal handover (median 111 s, about 22 polls), about 6% of each day. The wait job now starts the bridge workflow four minutes before each slot, and the bridge polls the same venues with the same rows and budgets until five minutes after it, into `data/<venue>/quotes-bridge/<day>/`, overlapping both of the chain's sessions. Its files go to release assets like the chain's quotes, with manifest lines in `manifests/bridge/<day>.ndjson`, so its commits never edit a file the chain edits. `data/<venue>/quotes/` is unchanged, so readers of that stream see exactly what they saw before; a reader that wants continuous quotes merges the two streams by time. A bridge that starts late or fails leaves the gap as before. Reason: FAITH's paper rules close a position over a gap of more than two polls, so without the bridge no position could be held past about 28 minutes, and the candidates still worth a paper test hold 1 to 4 hours.
- 2026-11-12c: offload and materialize hardened after a second review (no change to any row). (1) Each one-second bar chunk's manifest entry now carries its `offset` in the rebuilt day file; lines written before this get the offset their order implies, which holds because no bar file changed in git after the switch (checked 2026-11-12 05:20 UTC; all 1,906 chunks to then line up). (2) A bars offload now parks every day's chunks in `state/offload-retry/<path>` (committed) and puts the day files back as they were before any upload starts, so a failed, hung or killed run never leaves rows out of place in git; the next run sends the parked chunk first, joined to its own rows. Before taking offsets for a day, it also looks for bar assets no manifest line names and adopts each one whose chunks start where their files end, first cutting from its own parked rows anything the orphan already holds; anything else (a chunk at another offset, parked rows that diverge, an asset it cannot read, a listing error) holds the day's chunks parked, records the hold in `state/offload-held.json` and logs an error every run, and `--check` lists held days. An orphan from a day's last run is not looked at by any later run (none has rows for that day) and needs `--adopt` by hand; nothing can overtake it meanwhile. Before, a failed run committed its rows at the day file's path, and materialize would have put them ahead of earlier offloaded chunks. (3) `materialize.mjs` applies a chunk only where its file ends and only after checking the chunk before it (or the HEAD content) is in place, and skips a chunk already in place, so a lost `.materialized.json` or a second run never appends twice; an append cut off part way is finished. (4) Each bundle carries its own manifest line as `manifest.json`, and local copies go before the line is written: a run killed in between leaves an asset no line names, which `--check` lists and the new `--adopt` restores when its files are not already in git and its chunks fit. (5) Release and asset names in manifest lines are checked before use; an upload is also checked against the asset's sha256 digest where GitHub reports one, and an upload that fails its check is deleted; asset lists are read page by page. Quote files still fall back to git as before.
- 2026-11-12d: `recorder/coverage.mjs`, quote coverage per venue and UTC day estimated from session file names only (the manifests for offloaded days, the checkout for older ones), so it never opens a quote file and can run on days sealed for reading. A session counts from its start to the next start, at most 31 minutes. Days under 720 estimated minutes are flagged, which is the per-day bar FAITH's queue-prov-r3 needs for 30 days (it counts minutes with a poll, so this is an early warning, not its count). No change to any row or to the record job.
- 2026-11-12e: (1) the Binance.US quote session budget is 12 MB raw instead of 3 MB (`recorder/config.json`, and the default in `quotes.mjs`), a safety valve rather than a budget: a session writes at most about one row per pair per poll (259 pairs x 360 polls x 58 bytes, about 5.4 MB), so only a fault or a universe past about 550 pairs reaches it. Measured from session end rows and file sizes only: under 3 MB no session was capped (0 of 58 on 2026-11-10 and 11, largest 1.75 MB raw, median 1.34 MB; the only capped sessions were the first two on 2026-11-10, under the old 800 kB budget), and the largest of the 15 sessions of 2026-11-12 to 07:00 UTC is 310 kB gzipped in its manifest line, about 1.5 MB raw at the 4.7 ratio of earlier files, judged from the manifest size alone. Coinbase and Kraken keep 250 kB (largest 90 kB, at most about 110 kB). A half hour about 1.7 times as busy as the busiest of 2026-11-10 and 11 would have lost its changes after the cap; now it keeps them. Sessions go to release assets, not git, so the repository does not grow by it; a session at the new budget is about 3 MB gzipped, far under GitHub's 2 GiB per asset. (2) A size stream beside each quote stream, `data/<venue>/quotesz/<day>/<same name as the quotes session>` (bridge: `quotesz-bridge/`): `["z", ms, symbol, bid, bidQty, ask, askQty]` when neither price changed since the last poll but a size did, plus the session's `s`, `x`, `c` (its own budget, by default the quotes file's) and `e` rows. With the quotes file's `f` and `q` rows it gives every change of best price or size at poll resolution. It goes to release assets as its own kinds, `quotesz` (lines in `manifests/<day>.ndjson`) and `bridgesz` (lines in `manifests/bridge/<day>.ndjson`), through offload, materialize (`--check`, `--adopt`) and `coverage.mjs --stream quotesz`. `data/<venue>/quotes/` and `quotes-bridge/` are unchanged: the same rows, the same selection (a price change), the same file names and the same bundles; tests compare the quotes files of fixed synthetic runs with what the recorder wrote before this change, with the size stream on and off. FAITH's queue-prov-r3 reads `data/binanceus/quotes/` only and checks only `quotes` and `bridge` manifest lines, so what it reads does not change; the size files it finds rebuilt are listed in the manifests, as its clean-checkout rule needs. Estimated size from 2026-11-10 and 11 sessions (rows implied by sizes that moved between adjacent polls of the same pair, 58 bytes a row): Binance.US about 7,400 `z` rows a chain session, about 0.43 MB raw (21 MB raw, about 4.5 MB gzipped a day), at most about 63,000 rows (3.7 MB raw) if every pair-poll without a `q` row had one; bridge about a third more; Coinbase and Kraken about 1 MB raw a day together. About 96 more manifest lines a day (about 75 kB of text) are the only growth in git. Reason: queue models that need the displayed size at the touch had it only at price changes and 10-minute snapshots.
- 2026-11-12f: hardening of 2026-11-12e after review (no change to any row). A size-stream write that fails (a full disk, a path that cannot be made) now ends the size stream alone for the rest of the run, logged once, and never stops the quotes session or its `e` row: before, it threw out of the polling loop and could leave the quotes session unsealed and lose the next day's session at midnight. A test runs the fixed golden runs with the size directory unwritable from the start and from midnight on, and the quotes files still hash to the values of dedac40. In the record chain the size offload and its own commit now run after the quotes commit, with 1 and 2 minute timeouts, so a slow size step can hold back neither the quotes commit nor the next run by more than about 3 minutes (the wait job's timeout is 44 minutes, was 40); the bridge job's timeout is 22 minutes (was 16), its size offload 1 minute and its commit 3. `materialize.mjs --kinds quotes,bridge` (all kinds by default) rebuilds only the named kinds and downloads only their bundles, so a reader of the quotes streams alone, such as FAITH's queue-prov-r3, need not fetch the size bundles; its offsets are worked out over every line, so a filter never moves a bar chunk.
